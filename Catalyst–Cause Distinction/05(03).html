Why Banning the Spark Leaves the Fire

If a trigger only releases stored tension, then removing the trigger cannot remove the tension. This final reading follows that logic to its practical, uncomfortable conclusion.

Why do blowups keep happening even after we deal with the thing that caused the last one?

Fig. 3 — the fuel remains; only the spark changes.

the rule that didn't work

A rule gets made. No more leaving dishes in the sink. For a while, it works — and then, a week later, the same kind of explosion happens over something else entirely.

The rule wasn't wrong. It just aimed at the wrong target. It fixed the spark and left the fuel exactly where it was.

This is what happens whenever an intervention treats the trigger as the cause: it succeeds at the one thing it was never really solving.

fuel vs. spark

The Fuel Doesn't Care Which Spark Lit It

Interventions aimed only at the catalyst are superficial by design. They leave the underlying strain fully intact, still searching for its next outlet. The category of problem returns — only its surface changes.

What it looks like we've done

We fixed the problem. This exact thing won't happen again.

What's actually true

We closed one exit. The pressure is still there, looking for the next one.

same fire, different spark

How This Shows Up

Trigger-only fixes tend to leave the same kind of trace behind.

  • A new household rule prevents last week's argument — then an almost identical argument breaks out over something unrelated.
  • A team removes one process step after a conflict, and a near-identical conflict resurfaces a month later under a different name.
  • Banning a specific word or tool, rather than asking why it became a flashpoint at all.
  • A growing list of hyper-specific rules, each one written to prevent a fight that has already happened.
the honest fix

True resolution comes from discharging the tension underneath — not from policing whichever spark happened to light it last.

The Principle Underneath

This is the operational consequence of the principle running through the whole sequence. Because a trigger releases rather than creates, no amount of trigger-management can resolve what is actually a fuel problem. Understanding this doesn't hand you a fix — it tells you where to actually look.

Triggers release energy. They do not generate it.
what to do with this

What You Now Understand

Removing a trigger stops one moment from repeating. It does nothing to the tension that produced it — which will simply find its next spark.

Sit with this

Think of a rule, habit, or fix you've made after a blowup. Be honest: did it address what was building underneath, or only the specific thing that lit it that time?
