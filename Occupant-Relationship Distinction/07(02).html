Changing Who Holds Power Does Not Eliminate the Hierarchy

A hierarchy is defined by the relationship between its positions, not only by who occupies them. Replace the gatekeeper and the gate can still work exactly as it did.

Central question

If the people holding power change, does the relationship through which that power is exercised necessarily change with them?

Fig. 2 — the gate survives the gatekeeper. Only the hand on the lock is different.

Where you may recognize this

This is easiest to notice from the outside, in a single reversal:

  • A building has one entrance and one gatekeeper, who has long let one group through while restricting another.
  • The excluded group finally gains control of the entrance — and begins imposing the same kind of restriction on the group that once restricted them.
  • People say “at least it's not them in charge anymore,” as if that settled the question of fairness.

The structure, not the name on the door

Picture a building with a single entrance and a gatekeeper who, for years, let one group in freely while placing extra restrictions on another. The excluded group eventually takes control of the entrance. What happens next depends entirely on the principle the new gatekeeper governs by — not on which group now holds the keys.

If the new gatekeeper opens access fairly, the relationship has changed. If the new gatekeeper imposes the old restriction on the old favoured group, the identities have swapped but the relationship remains recognisable: there is still a gatekeeper, still a group whose entry depends on another group's approval.

What has changed

The person standing at the gate. Who currently benefits from the arrangement.

What may not have changed

The existence of a gate at all. The fact that one group's access still depends on another group's permission.

What becomes visible

That a new occupant is not, on its own, evidence that an arrangement has become fair. This does not mean every change of power is a hierarchy reversal — only that occupant change and relationship change are two different questions, and answering the first does not answer the second.

Where this fits

In the manifestation

This is the structural heart of the manifestation — the clearest direct statement of how dominance can survive a change of occupants.

In the model

It is the fullest expression of the Occupant–Relationship Distinction: the Occupants and Relationship lenses applied together to a single mechanism.

Before & after

Reader understanding

Changing who exercises authority does not necessarily eliminate the authority's shape. The relationship itself — who answers to whom, on what terms — is what has to change.
