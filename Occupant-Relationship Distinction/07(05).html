Historical Injustice Does Not Remove the Need for Individual Fairness

A group's historical position does not automatically determine the moral responsibility of every individual who happens to belong to it, in either direction.

Central question

Can we acknowledge historical group disadvantage while still judging individuals fairly?

Fig. 5 — the group's history explains why correction is needed. It does not stand trial for the person in front of you today.

Where you may recognize this

This tension surfaces whenever a group's past is used to settle a question about one person in the present:

  • Someone is treated as personally responsible for a historical injustice solely because of the group they belong to.
  • Someone else is excused from any responsibility at all, solely because of theirs.
  • “People like you have always had it easier” is offered as a complete answer to what this person, specifically, deserves.

Two levels that do not automatically align

Groups can carry long-standing structural disadvantages that deserve recognition and correction. But a group's historical experience and an individual's personal responsibility operate at different levels.

A person can belong to a historically advantaged group without having created or equally benefited from the original injustice. A person can belong to a historically disadvantaged group without that membership automatically justifying every action taken in its name.

This distinction does not deny collective disadvantage. It only insists that recognising a group's history and judging an individual's circumstances are two separate acts, both of which still have to be done.

Group history

Explains why a corrective measure may be necessary at all. A pattern, not a person.

Individual circumstance

What this specific person actually did, benefited from, or is responsible for.

What becomes visible

The past explains why correction may be necessary. It does not, by itself, determine whether every proposed correction is justified — that still depends on examining what is actually being addressed, and who, specifically, it affects.

Reader understanding

Historical injustice deserves correction. But individual fairness has to remain part of that correction — group history is a reason to act, not a verdict on any one person.
